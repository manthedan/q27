# DwarfStar4 (ds4) survey — techniques applicable to the Metal roadmap

2026-07-15. Survey of antirez's DwarfStar4 (`github.com/antirez/ds4`), a
Metal-first, single-model inference engine for DeepSeek V4 Flash/PRO —
architecturally the closest published cousin to q27 (narrow engine, one model
family, official-vector validation, Metal primary / CUDA secondary, agentic
serving as the headline use case). Analyzed at a shallow clone of `main`
taken 2026-07-15; file:line refs below are against that snapshot (local copy
was under the session job dir; re-clone to verify). Two deep passes: host
execution model (`ds4_metal.m`, ~27k lines, plus the graph driver in `ds4.c`)
and kernels (`metal/*.metal`).

**Verdict up front:** nothing here invalidates our roadmap; two items close
recorded gaps cheaply (overlapping buffer views, serving/KV-disk design), one
challenges a structural constant (prefill chunk 12 vs their 4096), and their
kernel evidence answers our chunk-GEMM "untried levers" question directly
(half staging: yes; double-buffering: nobody needed it; direct-RHS is the
documented win). Their MTP verdict ("experimental, at most a slight speedup")
independently corroborates that our Metal MTP parity problem is sync/readback
overhead, not drafter quality.

Ranked by leverage against METAL_PROGRESS critical-path items.

---

## 1. Overlapping model-buffer views — wire the official 17 GiB artifact on the 24 GB machine

**Our gap (recorded):** the whole-mapping + residency-set path (Ternary Phase
4 entry) only engages when the artifact fits `maxBufferLength` (13.3 GiB on
the 24 GB M4); the official 16.5 GiB tier "keeps per-tensor views and stays
unwired" — it still pays launch fault-in inside the timed window and cannot
join a residency set.

**Their solution** (`ds4_metal.m:1455–1597`, `ds4_gpu_add_model_view_range`):
wrap the mmap'd tensor region as a **small number of large, page-aligned,
overlapping `newBufferWithBytesNoCopy` views** — adjacent views overlap by
(max-tensor-size + one page), so every tensor lies wholly inside at least one
view and hot paths still bind one buffer + inner offset. All views join one
`MTLResidencySet` (`ds4_metal.m:1408–1453`), optionally attached to the queue.
They run an 81 GB model this way.

**For q27:** the official artifact needs two ~8–13 GiB overlapping views.
Loader change is localized to `metal_backend.mm`'s mapping setup; per-tensor
routing becomes (view index, offset) instead of (tensor view). Codex finding
2's fix (per-tensor logical lengths in `BackendTensor`) composes cleanly —
keep logical extents for `check_range` regardless of the physical view.

Gates: `make test-metal`, official-artifact canonical 16-token CUDA gate
byte-exact, "model ready" time moves to load (expect a few seconds like the
T2 path), steady-state decode unchanged, and a cold-launch A/B showing the
first-tokens fault-in tax gone on the official tier.

Also worth copying: their **coarse warmup dispatch** — a `kernel_touch_u8_stride`
pass over each view at 1 MiB stride at load, paying page-table
validation/first-touch cost outside the timed path, deliberately *not* a full
prefetch to avoid pressure stalls (`ds4_metal.m:1620–1634, 2000+`). Cheap
fallback for any mapping that can't join a residency set.

## 2. Prefill chunk size: they run 4096 tokens/chunk; we run 12

**The structural divergence.** ds4 prefills in 4096-token chunks
(`ds4.c:8346–8347`; PRO tier 8192), encoding **all 61 layers of the whole
chunk into one command buffer through a single persistent compute encoder**
(`ds4.c:20310–20412`) — no CPU turnaround inside a chunk. Our layer-major
chunk is 2–12 tokens, tied historically to the 1–12-row GEMM primitive and
MTP verify width (`W_MAX=12`).

Why it matters here: our prefill is **compute-bound** (Ternary Phase 2 note)
with the chunk GEMM at ~25 GB/s effective weight stream. Rows-per-weight-byte
is capped at 12; every staged weight tile amortizes over at most 12
activations. Raising the chunk to 128–256 multiplies arithmetic intensity per
weight byte without touching kernel inner loops. Nothing in the layer-major
path fundamentally requires 12: the GDN chunk kernel is sequential-in-kernel
over T regardless of T; chunk-causal attention generalizes (GQA-blocked
variant already handles the straddle/split contract); activation memory at
256 tokens × 5120 × f16 is trivial. MTP verify keeps its own width — chunk
width for prompt ingestion and verify width are separate knobs and should be
decoupled.

Their empirical support: GEMM token-tile of **128 retained after a 64-token
retest was "neutral or slower"** (`dense.metal:1044–1046`).

**Scope note — chunk attention changes shape at wide chunks.** At chunk 12,
chunk-causal attention is <2% of chunk time (our attribution) and our
one-threadgroup-per-(query-head, chunk-token) scalar kernel is fine. At chunk
128+ it stops being negligible, and the right structure is ds4's prefill
flash-attention shape (`flash_attn.metal:279–928`): a **block of Q queries
per threadgroup**, QK^T and PV computed with `simdgroup_multiply_accumulate`
over half-staged K/V tiles, so each K/V tile loaded from memory amortizes
across the whole query block through the matrix units — versus our per-token
scalar dots, where cross-token K/V amortization happens only through the
cache. Ride-alongs from their design when we get there: pad the final
partial K/V block to a full chunk so the kernel always reads full tiles
(`kernel_flash_attn_ext_pad`), and pre-scan for fully-masked/empty blocks to
skip them (`_blk` pre-pass) — the latter matters for causal chunks where
early tokens see few keys. Budget the attention rewrite as part of this item,
not a follow-up: the bit-exact chunk↔serial parity contract needs a
tolerance-gated variant or a parity redefinition at MMA shapes, same class of
decision as the chunk GEMM's 3e-4 gates.

Gates: chunked-vs-serial artifact A/B identical committed tokens (existing
gate), prefill-chunk ceiling bench (`metal_prefill_bench --chunk N` sweep),
and the op-level GEMM tolerance gates at the new shapes. Expect this to
compound with item 3.

## 3. Chunk-GEMM feeding — their evidence on our exact "untried levers"

METAL_PROGRESS lists "half-precision staging and double-buffered K-tiles" as
the untried levers for the ~25 GB/s chunk GEMM. ds4's kernels answer both:

- **Half staging: universal.** All three of their GEMM paths stage weight
  tiles in threadgroup memory as `half` with `float` accumulators
  (`simdgroup_float8x8` over half 8×8 fragments; classic path
  `dense.metal:1156–1358`, tiles 64 rows × 32 tokens × 32 K). We stage raw
  dequantized integers; half staging halves staged bytes and threadgroup
  traffic. (Numerics: our chunk-GEMM op gates are already tolerance-based
  (3e-4) with end-to-end exactness carried by the artifact gates, so a
  staging-precision change rides the existing gate structure.)
- **Double-buffering: absent from the entire codebase.** Their win came from
  the opposite direction: the documented fastest path
  (`kernel_mul_mm_mpp_direct_rhs`, `dense.metal:1040–1144`) **stages only the
  weight tile and reads the activation matrix directly from device memory**
  ("that direct-RHS shape was the clear win" — their comment), freeing
  threadgroup memory for larger weight tiles. That kernel uses Metal-4
  cooperative tensors (M5/M6-gated, not our M4s), but the *structure* — don't
  stage the RHS, spend all threadgroup memory on weights — transfers to plain
  `simdgroup_matrix` code.
- **Function-constant specialization:** bounds checks compiled out of
  interior tiles via `MTLFunctionConstantValues` (separate edge-tile pipeline
  variants, `dense.metal:917–918`); simdgroup counts, strides, and feature
  flags are all specialization constants across their kernels. Our
  runtime-compiled shaders make this trivially adoptable; today our kernels
  carry these as runtime parameters/branches.
- Minor: `simdgroup_barrier(mem_none)` between MMA sub-steps instead of full
  threadgroup barriers (`dense.metal:1294–1306`); pointer-form threadgroup
  stores to dodge a slow Apple compiler address-lowering path
  (`dense.metal:1253–1254` — codegen workaround found empirically).
- Marginal, chunk-width-dependent: they fuse the SwiGLU elementwise math into
  the matvec **epilogue** (the lane that reduced the row computes
  `silu(gate)*up` before writing; `moe.metal:131–135`,
  `kernel_dsv4_shared_gate_up_swiglu_q8_0` in `dense.metal`) and store mid
  activations as half. Distinct from the fused-*pair-GEMV* we measured and
  rejected for register pressure — this fuses only the epilogue elementwise
  step. Noise on serial decode (~5 ms "everything else" of a 218 ms token);
  re-evaluate only if wide-chunk profiles show the separate SwiGLU/mid-write
  dispatches gaining share.

**Recommended order:** half staging + bigger tiles (with item 2's wider
chunks) + direct-device RHS reads, measured on `metal_prefill_bench`;
deprioritize double-buffering — a sister engine at the same problem never
needed it. Keep our integer-exact weight side only if the tolerance gates
demand it; theirs is half throughout with float accumulation.

## 4. Per-token readback and GPU sampling — corroboration, plus one move past them

**Their pattern:** the greedy/speculative path never reads full logits for
intermediate rows. A GPU argmax (top-k=1 via bitonic argsort,
`ds4_metal.m:12648–12660`) writes an int32; the host reads **4 bytes**
(`ds4.c:19893–19922`, design intent stated in the comment: intermediate
verify rows stay device-resident, only the committed row does a large
readback). MTP draft steps likewise read 4 bytes per token
(`ds4.c:20016–20028`); the CPU acceptance walk compares those tokens. Full
600 KB+ logits are read back **only for the committed row** so sampling can
continue. Their *sampled* decode path still does full-readback + CPU sampling
every token — i.e., our GPU-sampling stage 2 plan (radix-select top-k +
candidate-list CPU sampling, HANDOFF-2026-07-15) already goes past them, and
our `q27_topk_logits` radix select is a better primitive than their argsort.

**The move neither engine has made — GPU-resident token feedback.** With GPU
argmax landed, have the embedding lookup read the token id from the device
buffer the argmax wrote. Then K greedy decode steps encode into **one command
buffer with zero CPU syncs**, reading back only every K tokens (committed
text + stop-check on the host, accepting ≤K−1 tokens of overshoot on stop).
This is the true "onegraph" for greedy decode and directly attacks the
ternary tier's remaining gap, which Phase 4 attribution pinned on argmax
readback sync + command-buffer turnaround (~8.2–9.4 observed vs 10.57–12.66
same-moment ceiling). Pieces already exist: embedding kernel, GPU argmax,
one-CB-per-step batching. MTP/suffix and sampled paths keep their current
round structure; this is the greedy/NLL/bench fast path first.

Gate: byte-identical committed tokens vs the per-step path over the canonical
prompts; ternary artifact decode re-measured back-to-back with the ceiling at
matched thermal state.

## 5. Serving layer — copy the disk-KV design nearly wholesale

Our serving-closure item (streaming landed; tool constraints, multi-slot,
compatibility suite remain) is where ds4 is most mature, and their design
answers problems we will hit with stateless agent clients (the Claude
Code/BUILDLOG use case where prefix reuse dominates wall time):

- **Disk-persistent KV snapshots** (README "Disk KV Cache"): keyed by SHA1 of
  the **rendered byte prefix**, with the tokenizer-decoded text stored in the
  file — a byte-prefix hit reuses the checkpoint even when the client's
  retokenization differs, tokenizing only the new suffix. Snapshots saved at
  four moments (cold / continued / evict / shutdown); **cold saves trim a
  32-token tail and align down to a 2048-token chunk boundary** specifically
  to dodge BPE-boundary retokenization misses when a future request appends
  text. Next-token logits are stored in the snapshot so resume needs no extra
  decode step — our in-memory snapshots already capture resident logits, so
  the extension is a serializer plus an LRU directory, not new engine state.
- **Exact tool-call byte replay:** a bounded map of tool-call id → the exact
  sampled tool-call bytes (persisted inside KV files), so when a stateless
  client returns normalized JSON tool history, the server re-renders the
  exact bytes the model sampled and the KV prefix stays valid.
  Canonicalization is the fallback only. The transferable principle for our
  constrained-tools work: **prefix-cache identity is byte identity** — any
  re-rendering drift silently forfeits the cache.
- **Greedy-for-syntax sampling:** temperature forced to 0 while the model
  emits tool-call structural syntax; the request's sampling restored for
  argument payloads (deterministic protocol without repetition artifacts in
  long file bodies). Composes with the constrain-tools plan
  (`plans/2026-07-07-constrain-tools.md`).
- Detail that rhymes with our paging saga: KV cache files use plain
  `read`/`write`, **not mmap**, to avoid adding VM mappings to a process that
  already maps the model.
- Context check: their server is also single-slot ("one live graph worker,
  concurrent requests wait") — our multi-slot ambition already exceeds
  theirs; nothing to import there.

## 6. Smaller items

- **`--power N` duty-cycle throttling** (`ds4.c:10497–10532`): EMA of GPU
  work time, proportional `nanosleep` between prefill layers / decode tokens;
  output unchanged, sustained load reduced. Given the gate-3 wall anomaly and
  the ceiling-vs-artifact thermal confound (hence the `caffeinate -dims` and
  matched-thermal-state protocol rules), a power knob for long runs on the
  daily-driver 24 GB machine is cheap insurance. Trivial port.
- **Split-K decode attention + merge kernel** (`flash_attn.metal` vec +
  `_vec_reduce`): partial (out, max, sum) per context-slice workgroup, merged
  by a second kernel. Structurally the same shape as our landed GQA-blocked
  kernels (grid over KV-head × 1024-block with merge) — independent
  confirmation, nothing new to take.
- **Command-buffer hygiene:** one persistent compute encoder across the whole
  graph (`ds4_metal.m:717–723`), `commandBufferWithUnretainedReferences`
  opt-in, optional `MTLResourceHazardTrackingModeUntracked` on model buffers.
  Our decode attribution says dispatch/sync overhead is a non-issue on the
  serial path, so these are only worth checking if profiling ever shows
  encoder setup in the per-step overhead residual.
- **MTLSharedEvent CPU/GPU overlap** exists in ds4 only for MoE
  expert-routing readback — but it's the right primitive if we ever want the
  host to consume partial results (e.g., streaming committed tokens) while a
  multi-token command buffer keeps running (item 4's K-step batches).

## 7. Distributed pipelined prefill — the two-M4 option

ds4 runs layer-split inference across machines over plain TCP: coordinator
owns tokenization/sampling and layers `0:N`, workers own the rest; **prefill
pipelines micro-batches across machines** (chunk N+1 on machine A while
machine B runs chunk N). Measured on two MacBooks over Thunderbolt: 1.38–1.85×
prefill scaling with prompt length; **generation is strictly slower** (−19.4%
— one network hop per autoregressive token). Robustness details worth noting
if we ever build this: rolling 64-bit token-prefix hash validated on every
work item (a restarted worker can't silently accept work at the wrong
position); KV rebuild by prefix replay on worker reconnect; activation
transport precision knob (their 16/8-bit transport experiment showed no
significant win — recorded as a negative result).

**For us:** we own exactly two M4 machines, and a layer split would put
~8.5 GiB of the official artifact on each — inside the mini's 8 GiB
`maxBufferLength` per-view limit with item 1's overlapping views, and inside
its memory budget where the whole artifact today is not. That would make the
official tier runnable on the mini at all, and could roughly double official
prefill throughput for agentic workloads (prefill dominates agentic wall time
per drafter-probe-plan.md). Honest sizing: this is a real multi-week project
(protocol, route management, per-layer KV ownership, failure handling), decode
gets slower, and the memory-safe policy applies on both ends. Someday-pile,
not critical path; revisit if official-tier agentic prefill becomes the
headline bottleneck. Cheap first step if we ever start: their `--debug`
per-hop telemetry format (layer range, local eval ms, downstream wait, socket
ms, byte counts) is the right diagnostic shape.

## Kernel head-to-head (summary)

Where their kernels beat ours, and vice versa — recorded so the survey isn't
misread as one-directional. The pattern: **theirs win where prefill/batch
throughput binds, ours win where decode bandwidth binds**, matching the two
projects' priorities (they serve huge prompts into an MoE; we chased the
decode ceiling first).

Theirs better:
1. **Prefill GEMM feeding** — half-staged weight tiles, 32–128-token tiles,
   direct-RHS variant, bounds-check-free interior tiles (items 2–3 above).
2. **Batched attention** — Q-block MMA tiling amortizes K/V through the
   matrix units (scope note in item 2); only matters once our chunks widen.
3. **Function-constant specialization** as a codebase-wide discipline.

Ours better:
1. **Decode GEMV** — their Q8_0 matvec is the per-block scalar-load +
   `simd_sum`-per-block shape (`dense.metal:108–176`) we measured as
   issue-bound and replaced with the packed-dot rewrite (84–90 GB/s); the
   ternary select-form GEMV (93 GB/s) has no counterpart.
2. **KV compression** — turbo3 dequantizes fused inside attention; their FP8
   KV is a quantize-dequantize round-trip stored back as F16
   (`dsv4_kv.metal:104,208`) — no fused compressed read at all.
3. **GPU top-k** — radix select vs their bitonic argsort; and their argsort
   never touches the vocab head (router/indexer only).
4. **Attribution tooling** — no bandwidth instrumentation exists in their
   source; `Q27_METAL_PROFILE` + the bench-replay pair have no equivalent.
5. **GDN/recurrent path** — no counterpart in their model family.

## Non-transfers (checked, excluded)

- **SSD expert streaming + hotlist** (`ds4_ssd.c`, `ds4_streaming_hotlist.inc`,
  mlock'd expert cache with lockable-margin fallback): routed-MoE-specific —
  a cold majority of weights to page. Our dense 27B has no equivalent cold
  set; the mmap/residency work already covers our version of this problem.
- **MLA/FP8/FP4 KV machinery** (`dsv4_kv.metal`): their FP8 is a
  quantize-dequantize round-trip stored back as F16 — attention reads plain
  F16 rows, decompression is *not* fused. turbo3 (fused on-the-fly 50-byte
  dequant in attention) is strictly more advanced; nothing to take.
- **Metal-4 TensorOps / cooperative tensors**: hardware-gated to M5/M6-class
  in their probe. Worth a probe-gated branch the day we benchmark on newer
  machines; not applicable to the M4 fleet.
- **Steering vectors, hyper-connections kernels**: model-specific /
  orthogonal.
- **GPU vocab sampling**: they don't have it — their argsort top-k serves the
  MoE router and the sparse-attention indexer, not the vocab head. Our
  stage-2 plan is ahead here.

## Cross-corroborations (no action, worth recording)

- Their MTP is shipped as "experimental… at most a slight speedup," gated by
  a confidence margin (`--mtp-margin`) to skip low-certainty drafts — the
  same conclusion as our Metal MTP-at-greedy-parity finding, from an
  independent codebase on the same hardware class. Consistent with the thesis
  that the Metal speculative win is gated on item 4's sync/readback removal,
  not drafter quality (our CUDA ladder with dexit/pmin already embodies the
  confidence-gate idea).
- Their validation stack (official-API logit vectors, pinned prefill chunk
  for strict comparisons, capability regression suite run on the real GGUF)
  is the same discipline as our CUDA-oracle gates + BUILDLOG task benches —
  and their vectors pin `DS4_METAL_PREFILL_CHUNK=2048` for strict comparisons
  because chunk size changes the checkpoint/logit path, matching our
  chunk-vs-serial low-margin-trajectory caveats.
- No GB/s instrumentation anywhere in their source; our
  `Q27_METAL_PROFILE` / bench-replay attribution tooling is ahead. Their only
  profiling hook is distributed `--debug` per-hop telemetry.

## Suggested sequencing against the current critical path

1. Item 1 (overlapping views) — small, closes a recorded official-tier gap,
   prerequisite-free.
2. Item 4 (GPU-resident greedy feedback loop) — attacks the measured ternary
   Phase 4 gap; stage-2 GPU sampling from the handoff doc is the natural
   entry point and is already scoped.
3. Items 2+3 together (wider chunks + GEMM staging rewrite) — the prefill
   lever; measure on `metal_prefill_bench` first, land behind the existing
   A/B gates.
4. Item 5 (disk KV + exact tool replay) — with serving closure, when tool
   constraints are picked up.
5. Items 6–7 as opportunity/appetite allows.
